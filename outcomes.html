Program Learning Outcomes

Seven Culinary Arts learning outcomes supported by real journal reflections.

Culinary Arts program

Seven learning outcomes

Each reflection connects an official Culinary Arts program outcome to a specific example from my education or internship. Supporting photos, certification coverage, and documented experience help illustrate these skills.

01

Culinary knowledge and technical skills

Program outcome: Integrate the knowledge, skills and attitudes in all areas of basic food preparation, advanced culinary arts, basic baking, nutrition, menu planning, guest services, and operational controls and management required to meet the requirements for a Certified Culinarian by the American Culinary Federation Foundation.

My reflection:

During my internship at Kāle's Hale, I applied the food-preparation and kitchen-management skills I learned in culinary school. My September 7–13 journal documents slicing pork, brisket, and tri-tip, wrapping laulau, making banchan and miso marinade, cutting fish, working the line, and helping in the dishpit. On October 11–17, I helped prepare approximately 600 bento orders and supported service at the ʻIlima Awards. These experiences taught me that consistent preparation, portioning, sanitation, and teamwork all affect the quality of the food customers receive.

Journal evidence: September 7–13 (prep, marinades and line service) and October 11–17 (high-volume bento production). See photographs from my internship.

02

Professional work ethic

Program outcome: Incorporate within their work ethic the standards in attendance, behavior, grooming and dress that reflect the mature work attitude expected of industry professionals.

My reflection:

My final internship reflection describes one of the responsibilities I am most proud of: Chef Kale trusted me to open the restaurant independently and get bento preparation started. That meant organizing the kitchen, preparing food, and making sure the operation was ready before service. Earlier in my internship, I practiced proper labeling, food rotation, cleaning, and restocking. These experiences taught me that a professional work ethic means being dependable, following food-safety standards, and doing what needs to be done even when a chef is not standing beside me.

Evidence: Final Internship Journal—independent opening and bento responsibilities; September 27–October 3—food handling and kitchen organization.

03

Critical and creative thinking

Program outcome: Make effective decisions with intellectual integrity to solve problems and/or achieve goals utilizing the skills of critical thinking, creative thinking, information literacy, and quantitative/symbolic reasoning.

My reflection:

In my September 13–19 journal, I noticed that laulau was regularly selling out, so I used available time to prepare a larger quantity for the following week. The next week, I made better use of downtime by preparing ingredients and cleaning before closing instead of waiting until the end of service. I also learned to think ahead when planning bento production. These decisions showed me that critical thinking in a kitchen means noticing a need early, setting priorities, and finding practical ways to keep the team ready.

Evidence: Internship journal reflections about preparing additional laulau, inventory, and using downtime productively.

Evidence: September 13–19—planning extra laulau for demand; September 20–26—using downtime effectively.

04

Effective communication

Program outcome: Ethically compose and convey creative and critical perspectives to an intended audience using visual, oral, written, social, and other forms of communication.

My reflection:

In my September 27–October 3 journal, I wrote about guiding newer coworkers through labeling, food rotation, removing spoiled or expired items, and storing meats correctly to prevent cross-contamination. Since some coworkers were new to working in a kitchen, I tried to lead by example and explain why each procedure mattered. During large bento orders and regular line service, I also practiced communicating about timing and shared responsibilities. I learned that good communication includes listening, teaching patiently, and making sure everyone understands the same standards.

Evidence: My journal account of helping coworkers with labeling, storage, and rotation; teamwork and service reflection.

Evidence: September 27–October 3—explaining safe food storage and rotation to coworkers; kitchen teamwork reflection.

05

Ethics, traditions, and community

Program outcome: Evaluate one's own ethics and traditions in relation to those of other peoples and embrace the diversity of human experience while actively engaging in local, regional, and global communities.

My reflection:

Preparing laulau gave me hands-on experience with a traditional Hawaiian dish and helped me appreciate the preparation and cultural significance behind it. Working at Kāle's Hale also introduced me to the way different food traditions and approaches come together in one restaurant. Outside the kitchen, I helped with community food events and a relief bake sale. These experiences reminded me to respect the people and traditions connected to the food I prepare and to use my culinary skills in ways that can support my community.

Evidence: Traditional laulau preparation at Kāle’s Hale and my community involvement.

Evidence: Final Internship Journal—traditional laulau preparation; community baking and volunteer activities.

06

Aesthetics and human experience

Program outcome: Through various modes of inquiry, demonstrate how aesthetics engage the human experience, revealing the interconnectedness of knowledge and life.

My reflection:

My August 25 journal describes learning different bento items and plating styles, including preparing bentos with another intern without the chefs directing each step. I became more aware of how portions, color, arrangement, and consistency influence a customer's experience. When preparing large bento orders, I learned that presentation still matters even when the kitchen is moving quickly. My culinary plating photographs show the attention to balance and detail I continued developing in school and at work.

Evidence: August 25—bento plating and presentation practice; culinary plating photo journal and prepared food photograph.

07

Connecting diverse perspectives

Program outcome: Explore and synthesize knowledge, attitudes and skills from a variety of cultural and academic perspectives to enhance our local and global communities.

My reflection:

My internship connected culinary school lessons to a working restaurant that prepares local-style dishes, bentos, and food for events. In my October 18–24 journal, I reflected on how Chef Aaron's organization and time management helped me plan ahead, while working with Jesse and Keanu taught me to anticipate what other teammates needed. Through community baking and Japanese culinary training, I also learned from experiences outside the restaurant. Bringing together these different perspectives helped me become more adaptable, collaborative, and confident in the kitchen.

Evidence: Japanese culinary training and community bake-sale coverage, alongside my internship reflections.

Evidence: October 18–24—teamwork with Chef Aaron, Jesse, and Keanu; culinary certification and community news coverage.

Additional artifacts: culinary plating gallery, internship and event experience, news coverage of culinary and community projects, and training and certification history.
